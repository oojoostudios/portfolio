# Remaining assets: what's best, what's acceptable, what's not

Each item has three tiers. **Best** is what a magazine art director would commission. **Acceptable**
is what you can do yourself in under an hour and I will place without reservation. **Not** is what
I would push back on, with the reason. A fourth line says what happens if you skip the item.

Order is by how much each one changes the site. Items 1 to 3 are the ones a hiring manager notices.

Deliver everything at the original size, uncropped, to the Drive folder. I crop, convert and place.

---

## 1. Portrait

**Why it matters.** It is the image a reviewer looks at longest, it reuses for years, and the
current one is a selfie-style snapshot in a round crop. Nothing else on the site says "junior" as
loudly.

| Tier | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Best | A photographer, one hour, environmental or plain wall, soft window light, landscape and square framings | $150 to $400 in San Francisco. Worth it. |
| Acceptable | A friend with a mirrorless or DSLR, 50mm or 85mm lens, you seated or standing near a window, camera at eye level, plain clothes, several framings | Ask for RAW or the largest JPEG. Twenty frames, pick two. |
| Acceptable | A recent iPhone in Portrait mode, same set-up, on a tripod or a steady surface, timer, window light, 2m back and zoomed to 2x | Turn off the Portrait blur afterwards if it looks painted on. |
| Not | A cropped group photo, a conference badge photo, a selfie at arm's length, anything with a ring-light catchlight, any AI-generated or AI-retouched face | A reviewer can tell, and a face is the one place that reads as a lie. |
| Skip | The current photo stays. It is the weakest asset on the site but it does not damage the case studies. | |

**Checks before you send.** Eyes sharp. No window reflection in glasses. Background a plain wall or
a room with depth, not a bookshelf arranged for the shot. At least 2,000 pixels on the short edge.

## 2. ReconLayer opener

**Why it matters.** The page opens on a monitor mockup with a white box around it. It is the
weakest opener of the three and the one where a real place would add the most.

| Tier | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Best | A photograph of your desk, morning light, the synthetic workbook and sidebar on screen, notebook with real handwriting, coffee; shot from seated height, slightly off axis, landscape, wide | Phone is fine if the light is good. Expose for the screen so it isn't blown out. |
| Acceptable | A composite: a stock or generated desk scene with a **blank** monitor, and your real screenshot placed on the screen in Figma or Photoshop with perspective matched | The screen pixels must be yours. Compose wide from the start, screen to one side, at least 3,000 pixels across. Send the layered file if you have it. |
| Acceptable | Keep the current monitor capture but re-export it without the white box, on transparent background, so it can sit on the project tint like the other figures | Ten minutes in the mockup tool. Smaller improvement, zero risk. |
| Not | An AI image where the generator redrew the screen | The text on the sheet will be wrong. I will check it against the real workbook before placing anything. |
| Skip | The current opener stays. Acceptable, not strong. | |

## 3. FXpress opener

**Why it matters.** The widget capture on a tinted ground is clean but it is a screenshot, and the
story opens on a sentence about your parents. The mockup is placed inline in Proof and works there.

| Tier | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Best | Your own hand or a parent's, your phone with the live widget, window light, shot from over the shoulder in landscape with room either side; 4,000 pixels or more | Ten minutes with a second phone. The honest version of the mockup you already made. |
| Acceptable | The mockup you have, re-exported from the tool at 3,000 pixels or wider, framed wide (phone off-centre, more background) rather than 4:3 | It then works at full bleed. The screen text is already verified. |

| Not | The 2,000-pixel version at full bleed | Soft on a Retina screen; you saw this already. |
| Not | An AI image where the generator redrew the screen | Same reason as ReconLayer. |

## 4. Homepage as a cover

**Why it matters.** The homepage is three identical SaaS-style panels. It is the first thing a
reviewer sees, and it is the last thing on the site that still reads as a template.

| Tier | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Best | One lead project at full bleed with its photograph and a one-line standfirst, the other two as a contents list with small images, then the about line | Needs item 2 or 3 in its Best or first Acceptable tier for the lead image. |
| Acceptable | Same layout, with the lead project's current opener as the cover image | Hwang has the strongest image today (the painting). FXpress is the better story for a product-design hire. Either works. |
| Not | Keeping three equal panels and adding motion to them | Motion on a template is still a template. |
| Skip | The current homepage stays. It is the single biggest remaining gap. | |

**What I need from you.** One word: which project leads. I would wait for a photograph and lead with
FXpress; if you want it done this week, lead with Hwang.

## 8. Process artifacts

**Why it matters.** One real notebook page or whiteboard photo per project shows thinking before it
was tidy. It is the thing senior portfolios have that templates don't.

| Tier | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Best | A photograph of a real page, whiteboard or printed wireframe with pen on it, from the project, shot flat in daylight | Don't clean it up. |
| Acceptable | A screenshot of the earliest Figma frame as it actually was, including the mess | Dated, if the file history shows it. |
| Not | A persona card, journey map, sticky-note wall or sketch made now for the portfolio | Reviewers recognise staged process instantly. |
| Skip | Nothing is lost. If it didn't happen on paper, the page is honest without it. | |

## Not needed, in any tier

- More screenshots of any product. Every figure slot is filled.
- A palette or type specimen page.
- Diagrams, flows or charts. These are built in the site from real data.
- Device mockups beyond the one already placed.
- A video of you presenting the work.
- Case-study PDFs.
