## 1. Three photographs

These are the single biggest lever left. A magazine feature opens on a photograph, not a screenshot,
because a photograph puts a person in the story. Right now two of the three case studies open on
UI captures and the About page has a round-cropped snapshot.

Shoot at the highest quality you have. RAW if the camera does it, otherwise the largest JPEG.
A recent phone in portrait mode is acceptable if the light is good. Deliver the uncropped original;
I crop. Aim for 4,000 pixels on the long edge or more.

### 1a. FXpress opener · a phone in a hand

**Where it goes.** Full-bleed under the FXpress title, 21:9. The current widget capture moves down
into the Proof chapter.

**What it has to say.** "Someone is about to send money home." The story's first sentence is about
your parents, so the honest image is a sender's hand holding a phone with the live widget on it.

**Set-up.**
- Open fxpress.netlify.app on the phone beforehand with USD to KRW and a real amount showing.
  Brightness up. Tilt the screen until there is no window reflection.
- Natural light from one side, near a window, late afternoon is kindest. No overhead lights.
- Shallow depth of field so the hand and screen are sharp and the background falls off.
- Landscape orientation. Leave empty space left and right of the hand; the crop is very wide.
- A little context is good: a table edge, a cup, a letter. Nothing branded, no other apps visible.
- If a parent will hold the phone, that is the right hand to photograph. Ask first, and ask whether
  they're comfortable with it being public.

**Avoid.** A phone lying flat on a desk photographed from above. A screen that is blown out. Any
device mockup or render.

### 1b. ReconLayer opener · a desk, early

**Where it goes.** Full-bleed under the ReconLayer title, 21:9.

**What it has to say.** "This is what a reconciliation morning looks like." The page opens with two
files, a spreadsheet and a cutoff, so the image is a desk with the sheet on screen and the signs of
someone working through it.

**Set-up.**
- Your own desk is fine. Monitor showing the synthetic workbook with the ReconLayer sidebar open.
- Shoot from a seated height, slightly to one side, so the screen has perspective and isn't a flat
  screenshot. The screen content does not need to be legible; it needs to be recognisable.
- Morning light. A notebook with real handwriting, a pen, a coffee. Keyboard in the foreground.
- Landscape, wide. Leave room left and right.

**Avoid.** A clean "setup" photo with matching accessories. It should look used.

### 1c. Portrait

**Where it goes.** About page, replacing the round crop, at a larger size. Possibly the homepage
cover and the social-preview image. This is the photograph a hiring manager will look at longest.

**Set-up.**
- Environmental: at a desk or in a room with some depth behind you, or against a plain wall.
  Soft, even light from a window. No flash.
- Eye level. Looking at the camera or just off it. Neutral expression or a small smile.
- Plain clothing, no busy patterns, no logos.
- Shoot both landscape and square framings. Head and shoulders, with air above the head.

**Recommendation.** Hire a photographer for an hour. Portraits are the one asset where the
difference between a professional and a friend with a good camera is visible at a glance, and the
image will be reused for years. If that isn't possible, a friend with a mirrorless camera and a
50mm lens near a window will do; a phone portrait mode is the fallback.
